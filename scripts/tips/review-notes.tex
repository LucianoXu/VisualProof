\section*{What we review}

The target is \lean{SpherePacking.Dim24.MainTheorem}: the densest packing of balls in
$\mathbb{R}^{24}$ is the Leech packing,
\[
  \lean{SpherePackingConstant}\ 24 = \lean{LeechPacking.density},
\]
and a periodic packing of that density is a scaled Leech packing. Lean checks the proof. What we
read is what the statement means: the definitions it rests on.

\section*{Its three keys}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \lean{SpherePackingConstant} $d$: the supremum of the densities of all sphere packings in
        $\mathbb{R}^d$, the constant the statement is about.
  \item \lean{LeechPacking}: the periodic packing of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ centred at the points of the
        Leech lattice, at separation $2$; its density is $\pi^{12}/12!$.
  \item \lean{IsometricToScaledLeech} $S$: some scaling of the Leech packing has the separation of
        $S$ and, up to an isometry of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$, its centres.
\end{itemize}

\section*{The proof, in three parts}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The lower bound is the Leech packing itself:\\
        \lean{LeechPacking\_density\_le\_SpherePackingConstant}.
  \item The upper bound is the Cohn--Elkies linear programming bound applied to the auxiliary
        function \lean{scaledF}: it comes to $\pi^{12}/12!$.\\
        \lean{SpherePackingConstant\_le\_leech\_density}.
  \item Uniqueness among periodic packings comes from the case of equality in that bound.
\end{itemize}

They are used in the proof alone, so none of them is in the TCB: Lean checks them.
