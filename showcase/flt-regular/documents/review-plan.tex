\section{What we review}

flt-regular proves Kummer's theorem: for an odd regular prime $p$ the equation
$a^p + b^p = c^p$ has no solution in nonzero integers. A prime $p$ is \emph{regular} when it does
not divide the class number of the cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$. In Lean:
\begin{code}
theorem flt_regular {p : ℕ} [Fact p.Prime] (hreg : IsRegularPrime p)
    (hodd : p ≠ 2) : FermatLastTheoremFor p
\end{code}
Lean has checked the proof. What nobody has checked for us is that the statement says this:
that \lean{IsRegularPrime} is regularity, that \lean{FermatLastTheoremFor} is Fermat's equation,
and that the definitions underneath mean what their names say. That is the statement's
\emph{trusted computing base} (TCB), and it is what we review, in VisualProof, declaration by
declaration.

\section{What we trust}

VisualProof trusts Lean's core and all of Mathlib by default. The statement's meaning, though,
rests on a handful of Mathlib's number-theory definitions: \lean{CyclotomicField},
\lean{NumberField.RingOfIntegers}, \lean{FermatLastTheoremFor}. \textbf{Proposal (Bob):} trust
every part of Mathlib this project uses except \lean{Mathlib.NumberTheory}, so that those
definitions come into our review.
